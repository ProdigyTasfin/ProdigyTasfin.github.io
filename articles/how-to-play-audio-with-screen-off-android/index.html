How to Play Audio With the Screen Off on Android

Learn how to keep music, podcasts and ambient audio playing after locking your Android screen, plus fixes for battery and background playback issues.

By Tasfin Hasan Sakib Published Jul 12, 2026 Updated Jul 25, 2026 4 min read

You start a podcast, lock your phone, and the audio continues playing. Later, you try the same with another app — and it stops the moment the screen turns off. Why?

Android supports screen-off audio playback, but not every app is designed to handle it the same way. This guide walks you through the most practical steps to keep music, podcasts, and ambient sound playing after you lock your screen.

We will cover app support, battery settings, lock-screen controls, common reasons audio stops, and a quick troubleshooting checklist.

Can Android play audio with the screen off?

Yes — Android can continue playing audio after the screen locks, provided the app you are using supports background media playback. When a compatible app plays audio, a media notification typically appears, and you can often control playback from the notification panel or lock screen.

Locking the screen does not turn off the phone — Android continues running, and properly designed audio apps can keep playing in the background. However, battery optimizations, app design, and manufacturer-specific settings can sometimes interrupt playback.

It is worth noting that screen-off playback is different from powering off the device completely. If you turn the phone off, all audio stops.

Use an app that supports background playback

The first requirement for screen-off audio is an app that is built to handle background media. Not all apps support this — some limit background playback based on their features, subscription model, or content licensing agreements.

Quick test:

  1. Open the audio app.
  2. Start a permitted music track, podcast, or ambient sound.
  3. Confirm that a media notification appears.
  4. Lock the screen.
  5. Wait 20–30 seconds.
  6. Unlock the phone and check whether playback continued.
  7. Test the lock-screen controls if available.
Practical tip

If playback stops only in one specific app, the limitation likely belongs to that app rather than Android itself. Some apps intentionally restrict background playback to encourage using their premium version or to comply with content licensing.

Check battery and background settings

Aggressive battery management is one of the most common reasons audio stops when the screen turns off. Android and device manufacturers use various power-saving features that can limit background activity.

General menu path: Settings → Apps → Select the audio app → Battery

Possible options may include:

  • Unrestricted — allows the app to run freely in the background, which may improve playback reliability but can increase battery consumption.
  • Optimized — the default setting for most apps, balancing performance and battery life.
  • Restricted — limits background activity, which may stop audio when the screen is locked.
  • Allow background activity — a toggle that may appear on some devices.

Other settings to check:

  • Battery Saver or Power Saving mode
  • Adaptive Battery
  • Sleeping apps or app standby
  • Background usage limits
  • Manufacturer-specific power management tools (e.g., Samsung's "Optimize battery usage," Xiaomi's "Mi Optimization," or Huawei's "PowerGenie")
Important

Change battery settings only for trusted audio apps that need continuous background playback. Allowing unrestricted background use may increase battery consumption. Do not disable battery optimization for every app on your device.

Use lock-screen and notification media controls

When audio is playing, a properly active media session typically shows controls in:

  • The notification panel (swipe down from the top of the screen)
  • The lock screen
  • Bluetooth headphones or car media controls
  • Supported wearable devices

Common controls include:

  • Play / Pause
  • Previous / Next track
  • Stop
  • Seek position or progress bar

Troubleshooting note: If audio plays but no media controls appear, check whether notifications are allowed for the audio app.

General path: Settings → Notifications → App notifications → Select the audio app → Ensure the notification toggle is enabled.

Why audio stops when the screen turns off

Here are the most common reasons audio may stop after you lock your Android screen:

  1. The app does not support background playback. Some apps are designed to stop when the screen locks.
  2. Battery restrictions stop background activity. Aggressive power management can pause or kill background processes.
  3. Battery Saver is active. Power-saving modes often limit background activity to extend battery life.
  4. The app's notification permission is disabled. Without a media notification, Android may not keep the audio process active.
  5. The network connection becomes unstable. Streaming requires a stable Wi-Fi or mobile data connection.
  6. The app is manually removed from recent apps. On some devices, swiping an app away from the recent-apps view can stop background audio.
  7. The phone manufacturer applies aggressive background limits. Some brands impose stricter background controls than stock Android.
  8. Bluetooth headphones disconnect. Audio may pause or stop when the connection is lost.
  9. A sleep timer reaches its end. Many audio apps include a timer that stops playback after a set period.
  10. Another app takes control of audio playback. A phone call, navigation voice, or another media app can interrupt or override the current audio.
  11. The content provider limits screen-off playback. Some services restrict background playback based on your subscription tier.

Quick troubleshooting checklist

Work through these steps in order to identify and fix the issue:

  1. Confirm that the app supports background playback. Check the app's settings or documentation.
  2. Start audio and look for a media notification. If no notification appears, check notification permissions.
  3. Lock the screen and test playback. Wait at least 30 seconds to ensure it is not a brief delay.
  4. Allow necessary notification categories. Enable media notifications for the app.
  5. Review the app's battery setting. Set it to Unrestricted or Optimized (not Restricted).
  6. Temporarily turn off Battery Saver for testing. If playback resumes, Battery Saver is the likely cause.
  7. Check Wi-Fi or mobile-data stability. A weak connection can interrupt streaming.
  8. Reconnect Bluetooth headphones if needed. Disconnect and reconnect to refresh the connection.
  9. Restart the app. Force-close and reopen the audio app.
  10. Restart the phone. A simple restart can resolve many background-process issues.
  11. Update the app and Android system. Ensure you have the latest versions installed.
  12. Test another legally permitted audio source. This helps determine whether the issue is with the app or the specific content.
Pro tip

Change one setting at a time and test playback after each change. This helps you identify exactly what fixed the problem.

Frequently asked questions

Can Android play music after the screen is locked?

Yes. Android can continue playing music after the screen is locked, provided the app supports background playback and Android allows the app to remain active in the background.

Why does my music stop when my Android screen turns off?

Common causes include app limitations, battery restrictions, Battery Saver mode, network issues, and manufacturer-specific background controls. Check the troubleshooting checklist above for step-by-step guidance.

Does screen-off audio use less battery?

Turning off the display usually reduces display-related battery consumption. However, streaming audio, Bluetooth, mobile data, and background processing still consume power. The overall battery impact depends on your device, settings, and what you are playing.

Can I control audio from the Android lock screen?

Yes. Supported media apps can provide playback controls through the lock screen and notification panel. These typically include play, pause, skip, and stop.

Can a sleep timer stop background audio automatically?

Yes. Many audio apps include a sleep timer that stops playback after a selected period. This can be useful for bedtime listening without needing to unlock your phone to stop playback.

Final thoughts

Screen-off audio is a practical Android feature that works well when the app, settings, and permissions are aligned. Start with a compatible app, check your battery and notification settings, and use the troubleshooting checklist to resolve common issues.

One action to try today: Open a supported audio app, start permitted audio, confirm that the media notification appears, lock the screen, and test playback for 30 seconds. If it works, you have successfully set up screen-off listening. If not, work through the checklist above — one step at a time.

Remember to respect the terms of the apps and services you use. Screen-off playback is a feature of Android, not a way to bypass content restrictions or platform limitations.
